Below is a focused scouting matrix for **rebar location / cover depth / diameter-radius estimation from GPR B-scans in concrete**, with emphasis on **physical inverse problems** rather than generic object detection.

## Bottom line

For your case — **single rebar, 2D cylindrical cross-section, B-scan in concrete** — the most reusable stack is:

1. **gprMax** for controlled 2D/3D FDTD forward simulation of a PEC cylindrical rebar, including direct parameter sweeps over radius, depth, permittivity, conductivity, antenna offset, and waveform. gprMax is GPL-3.0 and explicitly supports FDTD Maxwell simulation; its examples include a 2D PEC cylinder/rebar model with radius and grid-resolution guidance. ([GitHub][1])
2. **RGPR** or **lweileeds/hyperbola_recognition** for hyperbola picking/fitting, velocity/depth estimation, and migration baselines. RGPR directly returns hyperbola apex, RMS velocity, and depth from fitted hyperbolae. ([GitHub][2])
3. A custom inversion layer: Bayesian optimization / shuffled complex evolution / least-squares waveform matching / dictionary matching over gprMax simulations. The best published validation targets are **Jazayeri et al. FWI**, **Giannakis et al. ML physical-parameter inversion**, **Patsia et al. deep-learning forward-solver FWI**, and **Aziz & Alipour Bayesian-optimized FDTD**. ([Sajad Jazayeri][3])

The key warning: **depth and lateral location are much easier than diameter/radius**. Radius is weakly identifiable from simple hyperbola curvature alone because velocity, time-zero, antenna offset, wavelet, concrete dielectric properties, and target size are coupled. Later work explicitly argues that simultaneous velocity and target-size recovery from practical hyperbola fitting can be nonunique and noise-sensitive. ([Aberdeen Elsevier Pure][4])

## Practical interpretation for your project

### What is confidently identifiable?

**Lateral position and cover depth** are the low-hanging fruit. Hyperbola apex picking, migration, and velocity estimation are enough for a strong baseline. RGPR already gives you the fitted vertex, RMS velocity, and depth from selected hyperbola points. ([Emanuel Huber][23])

**Diameter/radius is identifiable only under stronger assumptions.** The most credible GPR-only diameter/radius methods use one of these:

* full waveform matching with source wavelet and concrete properties included,
* amplitude/polarization information,
* a calibrated simulation dictionary,
* global/Bayesian optimization over a forward model,
* or ML trained on physics-labeled synthetic data and validated on real slabs.

Curvature-only hyperbola fitting is the weakest route for radius because many combinations of velocity, time-zero, depth, and finite target radius can produce similar apparent hyperbolae. ([Aberdeen Elsevier Pure][4])

### Best reusable workflow

For a **single rebar / 2D cylindrical cross-section** project, I would build this pipeline:

1. **Forward simulation library**

   * Use gprMax with a 2D/3D model containing one PEC cylinder.
   * Sweep radius, cover depth, concrete permittivity/conductivity, Tx-Rx offset, waveform center frequency, and scan step.
   * Start with known radii such as 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16 mm and cover depths such as 20–150 mm.
   * gprMax examples already show how to represent a PEC cylinder/rebar and give grid-resolution guidance; for example, a 20 mm cylinder can be resolved with 2 mm cells in a high-frequency concrete-like example. ([gprMax Documentation][24])

2. **Geometry baseline**

   * Use RGPR or `lweileeds/hyperbola_recognition` to extract hyperbola apex, depth, and velocity.
   * Validate against Shihab/Ristić-style finite-radius hyperbola formulas.
   * Treat radius estimates from geometry-only fitting as a weak baseline, not the final answer. ([Emanuel Huber][23])

3. **Dictionary / global optimization layer**

   * Build a dictionary of simulated B-scans or hyperbola outlines indexed by `(depth, radius, εr, σ, wavelet parameters, Tx-Rx offset)`.
   * Match either the full B-scan window, the migrated image, the extracted hyperbola outline, or amplitude-normalized local patches.
   * Use Bayesian optimization or shuffled complex evolution for continuous parameters. Aziz & Alipour’s Bayesian-optimized FDTD workflow and Liu/Jazayeri-style FWI are the best conceptual templates. ([ScienceDirect][8])

4. **ML only after the physics baseline**

   * If speed matters, train a surrogate forward model or parameter regressor using gprMax simulations.
   * Keep the ML output physical: depth, radius/diameter, permittivity, maybe conductivity or moisture.
   * Giannakis et al. and Patsia et al. are the best examples because they estimate physical parameters rather than just bounding boxes. 

### Best validation targets

Use these as staged validation targets:

1. **Synthetic single-cylinder gprMax sweep**: validate location/depth first, then radius under known εr and known wavelet.
2. **Jazayeri et al. synthetic/real FWI benchmarks**: compare expected diameter error ranges and see if your inversion approaches their improvement from ray-only to waveform inversion. ([Sajad Jazayeri][3])
3. **Giannakis et al. single-A-scan setup**: validate whether the apex trace plus local B-scan patch carries enough radius signal under your antenna frequency. 
4. **Zatar et al. 2024 slab cases**: useful for practical #3–#8 bar diameter/depth validation with a 1.6 GHz antenna and known Tx-Rx offset. ([MDPI][15])

The cleanest implementation path is: **gprMax synthetic B-scans → RGPR/lweileeds hyperbola baseline → Bayesian/global waveform matching over depth/radius/εr → optional neural surrogate for speed**.
